ARISTO Hand: Sensing-Driven Distal Hyperextension for Fine-Grained Manipulation

Accepted to IEEE/ASME International Conference on Advanced Intelligent Mechatronics (AIM) 2026

Aaron Kim1, Dong Ho Kang1, Mark Helwig1, Mingyo Seo1, Kazuto Yokoyama2, Tetsuya Narita2, Luis Sentis1
1The University of Texas at Austin 2Sony Group Corporation

Abstract

Manipulation of thin objects requires precise contact geometry and reliable force perception, yet many anthropomorphic robotic hands lack the mechanical and sensing capabilities required for such interactions. We present the ARISTO Hand, a tendon-driven robotic hand that integrates active distal hyperextension with a hybrid fingertip sensing architecture combining a rigid nail-mounted force-torque sensor and a soft capacitive tactile array. Active hyperextension enables controlled fingertip engagement beyond the kinematic limits of standard flexion, increasing pull-out force by 2.76x for object thicknesses of 1-20 mm while preserving the nominal grasp capability. The rigid nail-mounted sensor provides reliable force measurements during edge contacts, where proprioceptive force estimation degrades due to Jacobian singularities. We validate the proposed architecture through quantitative force characterization and a multi-stage SD card extraction and insertion task.

Contributions

ARISTO stands for Anthropomorphic, Robotic, Integrated-Sensing, Tendon-Operated.

Distal Hyperextension

A tendon-driven distal hyperextension mechanism actively aligns the fingertip with flat surfaces for stable thin-object manipulation.

Rigid-Soft Sensing

A rigid nail-mounted force-torque sensor supports edge interaction while a compliant tactile pad monitors surface contact and slip.

Experimental Validation

Force characterization and SD-card manipulation show how fingertip geometry affects force transmission and contact observability.

Why Distal Hyperextension?

Small, thin, and fragile objects expose a gap in many dexterous hands: the hand must align a fingertip with a surface, detect subtle contact transitions, and generate secure engagement without crushing or losing the object. ARISTO addresses this by actively shaping contact at the distal phalanx instead of relying only on standard flexion.

The result is a hand that can flatten a fingertip against planar surfaces, place a rigid sensing nail into edge contacts, and still preserve the grasping behaviors expected from an anthropomorphic tendon-driven hand.

Mechanism Design

ARISTO uses an antagonist tendon path to drive the distal phalanx beyond a neutral extension posture. A dedicated routing path through the PIP joint, idler pulley, PIP motor, and tendon tensioners creates controlled distal hyperextension while maintaining the hand's nominal grasping workspace.

Hybrid Fingertip Sensing

The fingertip separates sensing roles across two contact surfaces. The rigid nail-mounted force-torque sensor measures edge and nail contacts with a direct load path, while the soft NARI-touch sensor captures distributed contact across the finger pad.

This hybrid layout is designed for interactions where a single sensing modality is ambiguous: the hand can sense nail contact, pad contact, and transitions between them during fine manipulation.

Force Characterization

The experiments quantify the core premise of the hand: changing distal posture changes the contact mechanics available to the robot. Hyperextension maintains high pull-out force over thin object thicknesses where non-hyperextended postures provide weaker engagement.

The paper also reports joint-level NIST metrics and transmission linearity experiments that characterize ARISTO's force-transparent, low-reduction design.

Nail Contact Observability

Thin-object tasks often begin with a fingernail-like contact, not a whole-pad grasp. ARISTO evaluates both normal and edge loading at the nail to show when the nail-mounted sensor captures clean contact signals and when motor effort becomes less reliable.

In low-velocity edge contact, the nail-mounted FT sensor maintains a pre-contact noise floor of 0.0004 Nm, while motor effort can be obscured by stiction. In normal-force tests near straight-finger configurations, motor effort can under-estimate contact force severely; targeting 3.1 N at 15 degrees produced 12.3 N of actual applied force.

SD Card Manipulation

The complete system is validated on a multi-stage SD card task: the hand locates and engages the card, extracts it from a constrained slot, transitions into a stable grasp, and reinserts the card. The task brings together the central ingredients of the design: distal hyperextension for access, nail sensing for edge contact, and soft tactile sensing for grasping.

The controller uses joint-space impedance control with compliant free-motion and high-stiffness constrained-interaction modes. Rigid fingernail contact is detected with manually selected force-torque thresholds, while the NARI-touch capacitive array monitors incipient slip during grasp acquisition and insertion.
